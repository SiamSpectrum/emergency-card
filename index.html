Emergency Card

A wallet card with the details that matter in the first minutes — blood group, allergies, medicines and two people to call. For an adult, a child, a parent, anyone.

Private by design: nothing is uploaded and there is no account or database. Your details are written into the QR code itself as plain text — the code is not a link and points nowhere. Any phone camera or scanner app reads the text straight out of the code, with no signal and no page to load, even on a phone that has never seen this page. Anyone who scans the QR can read the details, just like reading the card, so keep it somewhere sensible.

2 — Your card

Card language — useful when travelling, from all over the world. The form stays in English; the printed labels and the text inside the QR change to the language you pick.

Card design — eleven printed styles. The design changes the colours and header style only; the information and the QR stay exactly the same, and the design you pick is the one that prints and downloads.

What any scanner shows — no internet involved

        

        
This is the whole QR: plain text, not a web address. A scanner reads it directly from the printed squares — there is no page to load, so a first-time scan works in flight mode, in a basement, anywhere.

      

      
To print: use your browser or device Print option and choose 100% / Actual size, not “Fit to page” — only the two cards print, at true wallet size.

    

  


  

    
Before you carry it

    
    
      
  • Keep it current. Reprint the card whenever medicines or phone numbers change — an old card can mislead.
    • 
      
  • Check the QR once. Scan your printed card with any phone camera before you carry it — put the phone in flight mode first if you want to prove it. The scanner should show the details as plain text straight away: name, blood group, allergies, medicines and both phone numbers, with nothing to load and no signal needed.
    • 
      
  • One card, a few places. Wallet, school bag, glove box, inside a phone case — the same print works everywhere.
    • 
      
  • For a child, first name plus a contact is often enough on the outside; the fuller details live in the QR.
    • 
      
  • This is not medical advice and it does not replace an official medical ID — it is a simple, carry-everywhere summary you control.
